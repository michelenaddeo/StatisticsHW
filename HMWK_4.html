Exercise 1

Welford's recurrence formulas

One pass, one score at a time, no need to store the data. Notation: Mk mean of the first k scores, SSk = Σ(xi − Mk)², sk² sample variance.

1Derivation

Mean (Knuth). From kMk = (k−1)Mk−1 + xk:
Mk = Mk−1 + (xk − Mk−1) / k
Sum of squares. Use SSk = Σxi² − kMk². Subtracting the same expression for k−1:
SSk − SSk−1 = xk² − kMk² + (k−1)Mk−1²
Replace (k−1)Mk−1 = kMk − xk and kMk = (k−1)Mk−1 + xk: the right side collapses to
SSk = SSk−1 + (xk − Mk−1)(xk − Mk)
Variance. sk² = SSk/(k−1). Since xk − Mk = (k−1)/k · (xk − Mk−1):
sk² = (k−2)/(k−1) · sk−1² + (xk − Mk−1)² / k

2Code: three lines of core logic

n = mean = ss = 0
for x in data:
    n += 1
    d = x - mean            # x_k - M_(k-1)
    mean += d / n           # Knuth: M_k
    ss += d * (x - mean)    # Welford: SS_k
variance = ss / (n - 1)         # sample variance (n > 1)

The same three lines run in this page (JavaScript) for every demo below and for Exercise 2.

3Conclusions

Averages hit the target. The average of the sample means is close to μ and the average of the sample variances (divisor n−1) is close to σ², for every n: the estimators are unbiased.
Why n−1. Dividing SS by n instead underestimates σ² by the factor (n−1)/n: visible for n = 5 or 10, negligible for n = 1000.
More systems, less variability. The spread of the sample means falls like σ/√n and the spread of the sample variances also shrinks with n, so single estimates get closer to μ and σ².
Why it matters. With one sample we can estimate unknown μ and σ² and say how wrong we may be (confidence intervals, tests). The variance is estimated less precisely than the mean.
